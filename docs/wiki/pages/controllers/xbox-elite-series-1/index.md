# Xbox Elite Series 1

Pinned wired identity `045e:02e3` selects the original Elite xpad parser. Linux v7.3 advertises fifteen keys (including four grip paddles), eight axes and conditional main-motor force feedback; custom mapping suppresses independent paddle events. [`elite1-pa2-xpad-usb-identity`](https://github.com/anderstvoss/controller-protocol-research/blob/eb7887bf1aacc1b0baf6f5e973cac2ee41090c4e/records/claim/elite1-pa2-xpad-usb-identity.json), [`elite1-pa2-xpad-event-surface`](https://github.com/anderstvoss/controller-protocol-research/blob/eb7887bf1aacc1b0baf6f5e973cac2ee41090c4e/records/claim/elite1-pa2-xpad-event-surface.json), [`elite1-pa2-xpad-paddle-guard`](https://github.com/anderstvoss/controller-protocol-research/blob/eb7887bf1aacc1b0baf6f5e973cac2ee41090c4e/records/claim/elite1-pa2-xpad-paddle-guard.json).

SDL 3.2.0's database lacks explicit paddle slots and its legacy evdev paddle mapper expects trigger-happy keys rather than grip keys. A later pinned SDL mapper recognizes grip keys; native GIP HIDAPI uses an original-specific packet and remapping guard. [`elite1-pa2-sdl-base-db-entry`](https://github.com/anderstvoss/controller-protocol-research/blob/eb7887bf1aacc1b0baf6f5e973cac2ee41090c4e/records/claim/elite1-pa2-sdl-base-db-entry.json), [`elite1-pa2-sdl-linux-evdev-paddles`](https://github.com/anderstvoss/controller-protocol-research/blob/eb7887bf1aacc1b0baf6f5e973cac2ee41090c4e/records/claim/elite1-pa2-sdl-linux-evdev-paddles.json), [`elite1-pa2-sdl-grip-mapping-later`](https://github.com/anderstvoss/controller-protocol-research/blob/eb7887bf1aacc1b0baf6f5e973cac2ee41090c4e/records/claim/elite1-pa2-sdl-grip-mapping-later.json), [`elite1-pa2-sdl-gip-paddle-parser`](https://github.com/anderstvoss/controller-protocol-research/blob/eb7887bf1aacc1b0baf6f5e973cac2ee41090c4e/records/claim/elite1-pa2-sdl-gip-paddle-parser.json). Wired/proprietary receiver rather than Bluetooth is a planning inference, not proof of radio absence; physical and runtime gaps remain.
